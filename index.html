Overview

Capital is increasingly focused on climate adaptation and risk management, and there is no shortage of communities, utilities, hospitals, and companies that need to harden against climate risk. The money and the need are not finding each other with the urgency that the climate crisis demands.

Part of the reason is that resilience is hard to price. A mitigation project produces a loss that never happens, and an avoided loss does not appear on a balance sheet the way a revenue stream does. Underwriters, ratings agencies, and infrastructure investors are left estimating a benefit that no one has agreed how to measure.

The Resilience Capital Lab is working on that challenge. We are building the evidence base, the metrics, and the market infrastructure to help the case for climate adaptation and resilience be underwritten, rated, and financed.

Where the market breaks down

Two overarching challenges are behind a reluctance to fund climate adaptation and resilience. The first is knowing which variables matter. The second is causal evidence that a given investment produced a given financial outcome for the organization making the investment. A key starting point is to enumerate the variables that matter most, and to show how they played out in practice. From this, a more robust body of evidence can be built from current and evolving practices.

What we work on

Three workstreams, matched to the three things a market needs before it can function.

Workstream 01

Evidence

What actually reduces loss, by how much, and under what conditions. We work from observed outcomes rather than modeled ones wherever the data allows it.

  • Financial and policy case studies
  • Performance of mitigation in real events
  • Distributional effects across communities
Workstream 02

Metrics

Measurement that someone outside the room can check. Methods are published, inputs are documented, and results are reproducible.

  • Avoided-loss specification
  • Adaptation-adjusted risk scoring
  • Disclosure and reporting frameworks
Workstream 03

Market Infrastructure

The standards, reference terms, and convening work that let a transaction close without reinventing itself. Boring, and the reason nothing scales without it.

  • Reference deal structures
  • Standards and taxonomy engagement
  • Closed-door practitioner convenings
